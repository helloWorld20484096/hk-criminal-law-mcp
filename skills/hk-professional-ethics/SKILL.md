# HK Professional Ethics

> **Not legal advice.** This is a checklist for reflection and supervision.
> The governing rules and guidance of the relevant professional body prevail.

## 1. Who is the client?

- Identify the client precisely (individual, company, parent, insurer, legal
  aid-funded party).
- Confirm who gives instructions and who pays.
- Explain the scope and limits of the engagement in writing.
- If a third party is present, clarify confidentiality and privilege.

## 2. Conflicts

- Check conflicts before accepting the matter and throughout.
- Consider conflicts between co-defendants, current/former clients, family
  members, and the instructing solicitor/counsel.
- If a conflict arises, stop, disclose as permitted, and seek guidance.
- Do not use confidential information from a former client against them.

## 3. Confidentiality and privilege

- Criminal defence communications are generally privileged; verify the scope.
- Do not disclose client information without consent or a legal obligation.
- Beware of inadvertently copied third parties, shared devices, cloud storage,
  and AI tools that transmit privileged material.
- Do not discuss the case with the complainant, witnesses, or media.
- If a regulator or investigator requests information, seek advice before
  responding.

## 4. Competence and supervision

- Accept only matters within your competence or with appropriate supervision.
- Instruct counsel/expert where needed.
- Keep up to date with the law; record research and verification.
- Do not let unqualified staff give legal advice.

## 5. Candour to the court

- Do not mislead the court on law or fact.
- Disclose adverse authorities that are directly in point.
- Do not submit evidence you know to be false.
- Correct an error promptly.
- Do not make allegations without a proper basis.

## 6. Client perjury / false evidence

If the client insists on giving evidence you believe is false:
- advise against it;
- consider whether you can continue;
- follow the professional rules on withdrawal;
- do not coach the client to lie or tailor evidence to documents.

## 7. Evidence handling

- Do not destroy, alter, or conceal evidence.
- Preserve originals and metadata.
- Do not access privileged or unlawfully obtained material without advice.
- Be careful with witness contact; do not pressure or induce witnesses.

## 8. Money laundering / proceeds of crime

- Be alert to suspicious instructions, cash payments, or asset transfers.
- Verify the source of funds where required.
- Consider reporting obligations; do not tip off the client.
- Seek specialist advice if unsure.

## 9. Legal aid and fees

- Verify eligibility and the scope of legal aid.
- Do not charge the client for work covered by legal aid without authorization.
- Keep accurate time and disbursement records.
- Explain costs and fees clearly.

## 10. Media and publicity

- Do not try the case in the media.
- Do not disclose client information or comment on evidence.
- Consider reporting restrictions and anonymity orders.
- Advise the client on social media use.

## Ethics checklist

```
[ ] Client identified and scope confirmed in writing
[ ] Conflict check completed
[ ] Confidentiality/privilege considered
[ ] Competence and supervision addressed
[ ] Candour obligations considered
[ ] Evidence preservation and handling addressed
[ ] Money-laundering red flags checked
[ ] Legal aid/fees explained
[ ] Media/publicity risk considered
[ ] Advice and instructions recorded
[ ] Escalation: [senior lawyer / bar association / regulator]
```

## Escalation

Seek guidance from a senior lawyer, the relevant professional body, or
specialist counsel if the answer is not clear. Record the question and the
advice received.
